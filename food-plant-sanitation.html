Food Processing Plants

Sanitation for plants in PEI and the Maritimes

Chief Janitorial provides commercial sanitation for food processing plants. Crews follow a written plan aligned with CFIA, HACCP, and GMP expectations, on shifts that fit your production calendar.

This work supports your food-safety program. It does not replace your quality team, and it is not a claim of a specific certification or audit number.

  • Equipment and production-area washdown
  • Floors, drains, and walls
  • Night and weekend sanitation shifts
  • Sanitation logs and checklists

What We Do

Sanitation built around the plant

The walkthrough sets the scope. These are the pieces most food plants ask us to cover.

Program-aligned sanitation

Tasks are planned to line up with CFIA, HACCP, and GMP practices already in use at the plant.

Equipment washdown

Production equipment and the areas around it are washed down as the sanitation plan describes.

Floors, drains, and walls

Floors, drains, and walls are part of the regular scope, not an add-on left for later.

Nights and weekends

Crews can be scheduled when the line is down, including night and weekend shifts.

Logs and checklists

Each visit leaves sanitation logs and checklists so you can see what was completed.

Supervised, insured crews

Crews are trained and supervised, with workers’ compensation (WCB) and liability insurance.

How It Works

Walkthrough, plan, crews, records

01

Walkthrough

We walk the plant with you and note production areas, equipment, and the times sanitation can happen without getting in the way of the line.

02

Sanitation plan

You receive a plan for the tasks, the areas in scope, and how often they are done. Your food-safety program stays in charge of the requirements.

03

Scheduled crews

Trained, supervised crews arrive for the shifts you book, including nights and weekends when that is when the plant is available.

04

Documented results

Checklists and sanitation logs from the visit stay with you, so there is a record of the work that was completed.

FAQ

Questions plants usually ask

Where do you provide food plant sanitation?

Prince Edward Island and the Maritimes. The walkthrough confirms the plant, the areas in scope, and whether the schedule is a fit.

Are you CFIA, HACCP, or GMP certified?

Sanitation is carried out to align with those expectations. We do not claim a specific certification or audit number, and we do not replace your quality or food-safety team.

Can crews work nights or weekends?

Yes. Shifts are scheduled around production, including nights and weekends.

What areas are included?

Equipment and production-area washdown, plus floors, drains, and walls. Other zones are added only if the sanitation plan says so.

Do you leave sanitation records?

Yes. Crews complete sanitation logs and checklists for the work performed.

Are the crews supervised and insured?

Crews are trained and supervised. The work is covered by workers’ compensation (WCB) and liability insurance. Ask for certificates when you book. Policy numbers are not listed here.
